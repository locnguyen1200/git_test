The Beginning of Leica Snobbery

Intro

I’d never intended to obtain a Leica camera. After all, a film camera is just a light box. Hearing all the hype surrounding Leica and scrolling through Facebook Marketplace, I’d found one for a good price at the time, CLA’d and accessories included. Initially, not knowing much about Leicas, I asked the seller if this was a single-stroke or double-stroke model, the only distinction I knew for M3s. The seller, by the name of J, told me that he’d ONLY collected Leica M3s, and ONLY double-stroke models. Why? Well, that was for me to discover.

Specifications

Field Specification
Camera Type Rangefinder film camera
Film Format 35mm
Lens Mount Leica M-mount
Viewfinder 0.91x magnification
Shutter Cloth focal-plane (1s to 1/1000s + Bulb)
Film Advance Double stroke
Notable Features Bottom loading style, bright-line frame selector

Feeling Brass.

On the way back from the purchase, holding the camera in my hands, I instantly felt the heft of the brass body. Looking through the viewfinder, I saw bright, illuminated frame lines, providing a remarkably true-to-life perspective, nearly identical to viewing the world with my own naked eye. Then came the double-stroke: two deliberate pulls of the lever that rewarded me with a smooth, rhythmic whirring of gears, concluding in a crisp, surgical click. In that moment, my skepticism evaporated. I had walked into the deal expecting an overrated relic, but the tactile precision of that mechanism told a different story…one I was already falling for.
